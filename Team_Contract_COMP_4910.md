## 2 Workload, Timelines, and Quality Standards

### 2.1 Individual Workload Commitment

Each team member commits to contributing approximately 10–15 hours per week.

This represents an expected collective team capacity of approximately 40–60 hours per week.

Time will be distributed across independent tasks, technical implementation, meetings, documentation, testing, research, and project coordination.

### 2.2 Internal Deliverable Buffer

All assigned individual components should be completed and integrated 2–3 days before the official Moodle submission deadline.

This internal buffer is intended to provide sufficient time for:

- Peer review and feedback;
- Integration of individual components;
- Quality assurance and testing;
- Corrections and revisions; and
- Final submission preparation.

### 2.3 Schedule Conflicts and Notice

If a team member encounters an unexpected constraint, academic obligation, employment conflict, or other scheduling issue that may affect their commitment, they should notify the team through the group communication channel at least 24 hours in advance whenever reasonably possible.

The remaining team members will then determine whether tasks should be rebalanced or deadlines adjusted.

### 2.4 Quality Control

No deliverable will be considered complete solely because the assigned task has been submitted by an individual member.

All code, documentation, diagrams, presentations, and other deliverables must meet the team’s agreed quality standards before being considered final. A quality submission is one which meets the client’s needs and accomplishes the task assigned.

Incomplete or substandard work will be reviewed collaboratively during the Tuesday or Thursday team meetings and assigned for correction where necessary.

## 6 Dispute Resolution and Performance Management

The team recognizes that disagreements, scheduling challenges, and differences in workload may occur during a long-term project. Issues will therefore be addressed through a progressive process focused first on communication and resolution.

### 6.1 Breach of Agreement

Examples of conduct that may require intervention include:

- Unexcused non-attendance at required meetings;
- Failure to respond to team communications for more than 48 hours;
- Repeatedly missing agreed internal deadlines;
- Consistently submitting incomplete work; or
- Failure to communicate circumstances that materially affect assigned work.

### 6.2 Progressive Three-Step Resolution Process

#### 1. Direct 1-on-1 Check-in

The Project Lead or another designated team member will discuss the issue privately with the individual to identify blockers, clarify expectations, and establish a plan for returning to the agreed schedule.

#### 2. Full Team Mediation

If the issue remains unresolved, all four members will participate in a team discussion. The team will identify the underlying issue, rebalance workload where appropriate, and establish a clear recovery timeline, normally within 48 hours.

#### 3. Supervisor Escalation

If persistent non-performance continues despite the previous steps, the matter may be escalated to Academic Supervisor Latif. Relevant project records, such as Jira / Asana activity, Git commits, meeting attendance, and communication timestamps, may be provided to support an objective discussion of the situation.

### Resolution Principle

The purpose of the escalation process is to resolve project-impacting issues fairly and constructively while protecting the team’s ability to meet academic requirements and project commitments.

## 7 Accountability and Project Transparency

To maintain transparency throughout the project, work should be traceable through the team’s project-management and development tools.

Examples include:

- Assigned tasks and status updates in Jira / Asana;
- Git commits and pull requests;
- Meeting minutes and action items;
- Documentation revisions; and
- Team communication concerning major project decisions.

These records are intended primarily to support collaboration, progress tracking, and project continuity rather than to monitor individual members unnecessarily.
